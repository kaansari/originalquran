The most interesting **sound match** is Old Norse **geyja**, approximately **“GAY-ya / GAY-yah”**, but it means **“to bark, shout, scoff/decry.”** It comes from Proto-Germanic *gawjaną, so linguistically it is not considered related to Arabic **جاء**. There is also Old Norse **gjá**, pronounced approximately **“gyaw / g-yaw”**, meaning **a chasm, rift, or crack**. That is especially interesting next to Ibn Fāris’s **الجِئَة = a place where water collects**, because both can describe a **depression/opening where something may gather**, but this is a **semantic resemblance, not evidence of common origin**. [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/gj%C3%A1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

And Scandinavian **gå**, ultimately connected with Germanic words for **going/walking**, is semantically the closest to Arabic **جاء** because both deal with **movement**, although Arabic **جاء** is specifically “come/arrive,” while **gå/go** is “go/walk.” [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/g%C3%A5?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
