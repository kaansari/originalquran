The closest **HSN-looking Scandinavian form** I found is **Faroese _hesin_**, meaning **“this here.”** It comes from the Scandinavian demonstrative system and ultimately relates to Old Norse **þessi**, not to Arabic **حسن**. [Rus Family Wiki](https://wiki.rus.family/content/wiktionary_en_all_nopic_2025-07/hesin?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

There are also old Scandinavian dialect forms such as **hassen, hissin, issen**, likewise demonstratives meaning roughly **“this.”** A historical survey records forms including Faroese **hesin**, Kalix **hissin**, and Småland **hassen**. [Wikimedia Commons](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/11/The_old-northern_runic_monuments_of_Scandinavia_and_England_%28IA_gri_33125000703625%29.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For **Old Norse**, the strongest H-S sounding lexical item is **háss / háss**, “hoarse, husky.” It is historically connected with Old English and Old High German forms and ultimately Proto-Germanic **\*haisaz**. [Old Norse Dictionary](https://oldnorsedictionary.com/home/word/hass?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

So I would classify this as a **phonetic parallel**, not currently a demonstrated historical relationship.
