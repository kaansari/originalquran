# رحم

Rahm in Alemani/German, mercy; compassion; affection; wombs; kinship
Arabic **رَحْمَة (raḥma)** expresses **mercy, kindness, tenderness, and gentle care**. German **Rahm** means **cream**—the rich, **soft, smooth, and gentle-textured** part of milk.

This creates an interesting sound-and-meaning parallel:

**Raḥma** → kindness, tenderness, softness in treatment  
**Rahm** → cream, something physically soft, smooth, and rich

The image becomes even more suggestive because cream comes from **milk**, and milk is naturally associated with **a mother nourishing and caring for her child**. This brings in the Arabic word **رَحِم (raḥim)**, “womb,” from the same Arabic root **R-Ḥ-M** as *raḥma*. The expression **صِلَةُ الرَّحِم (ṣilat al-raḥim)** literally means maintaining the bonds of the womb—that is, maintaining **family and kinship ties**.

**mother → womb → milk → nourishment → tenderness → mercy**

This makes the resemblance between German **Rahm** “cream” and Arabic **R-Ḥ-M** especially striking at the level of imagery and meaning.
