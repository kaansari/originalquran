Yes — **gene / genetic** is a much more interesting comparison with Arabic **ج ن ن (J-N-N)**, especially semantically.

English **gene** ultimately comes through German **Gen**, coined from Greek **geneá** “generation, race,” from the Proto-Indo-European root ***ǵenh₁- / *gene-*** meaning **“to give birth, beget, produce.”** The same root produced **genesis, generate, generation, genus, genetics**, and is also related to Germanic words such as **kin** and **kind**. [Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/genetic?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Compare that with Arabic **ج ن ن**:

| Form | Basic idea |
|---|---|
| **جَنِين janīn** | fetus, something concealed in the womb |
| **جَنَّ / J-N-N** | to cover, conceal |
| **جِنّ jinn** | concealed/unseen beings |
| **جَنّة jannah** | a garden whose ground is covered by vegetation |
| English **gene** | hereditary unit connected with generation/birth |
| Greek **geneá** | generation, family, race |
| PIE ***gene-*** | give birth, beget |

So **جنين janīn** is especially striking:

**janīn = fetus / unborn child**  
**gene = hereditary material passed in generation/reproduction**

Phonetically:

**JAN / JIN / JNN**  
versus  
**GEN / GENE**

And there is an even stronger historical comparison outside Arabic:

- Sanskrit **jánati** = “begets, gives birth”
- Sanskrit **jana** = offspring/person
- Greek **genos** = race, stock, kind
- Latin **genus** = race, family, descent
- Gothic **kuni** = race
- Old English **cennan** = beget/create
- Old High German **kind** = child [Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/gens?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

So you get a remarkable cluster:

**JAN / GEN / KIN → birth, offspring, family, generation**

Arabic **جَنِين — janīn** fits that semantic field extremely well because a **janīn is what is generated/born**, although Arabic explains the word internally from **ج ن ن = concealment**, i.e. **the concealed one in the womb**.

Therefore I would distinguish two claims:

**Historical linguistics:** there is currently no established scholarly derivation of Arabic **J-N-N** from PIE ***gene-***.

**Comparative observation:** **janīn / gene / genos / jana / kin** have unusually close sound patterns **and** all intersect around **birth, offspring, generation, and ancestry**.

That is much stronger than the earlier **gína / ginn** comparison.

A particularly interesting next step would be comparing **Arabic جَنِين (janīn)** directly with Sanskrit **janin / janman / jana**, because Sanskrit **jan-** means **birth/begetting** and gets extremely close both phonetically and semantically.
