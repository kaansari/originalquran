# رزق
Risk
provision; providing; provider

**Rizq and risk:**

Proposed connection: Arabic رِزْق (rizq) → medieval Latin resicum → Italian forms such as risco/rischio → French risque → English risk. The Arabic origin is a hypothesis, not an uncontested etymology.

Sarina Kuersteiner's 2026 Speculum article studies Judeo-Arabic and Latin commercial evidence from 1154–1164. Its abstract identifies a 1156 contract by the Genoese notary Giovanni Scriba as the earlsiest documented occurrence of resicum.

Judeo-Arabic merchants used بما رزق الله (bimā razaqa Allāh), referring to what God provides. Latin notaries used quod deus dederit, expressing what God would give. Kuersteiner argues that Scriba's later use of ad tuum resicum reflects borrowing for an idea already present in commercial language.

The proposed semantic shift runs from divine provision or a venture's outcome toward commercial uncertainty. The article distinguishes early resicum from its insurance-related use around 1400.

Merriam-Webster traces English risk through French risque to Italian risco. Treccani describes the etymology of Italian risico as uncertain. These dictionary entries should be distinguished from the newer Arabic-origin argument.

The contrast is between rizq as provision or what one receives and the modern emphasis of risk on possible harm or loss. This historical proposal does not make risk a translation of the Quranic word rizq.

References: [Whatever God Gives: Arabic and Judeo-Arabic Rizq and Latin Resicum in Commercial Vocabulary, 1154–1164 CE](https://doi.org/10.1086/741592); [Risk — Word History](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/risk); [Risico](https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/risico/).
