The Old Norse connection is especially strong. **bani** literally means “death” or “a killer/slayer.” Cleasby-Vigfusson gives it as “bane, death” and then “a slayer.” [Germanic Lexicon Project](https://www.germanic-lexicon-project.org/html/oi_cleasbyvigfusson/b0051.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Old Norse also has the verb **bana**, meaning **“to kill.”** [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/bana?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

So you get:

**BANI → death / killer**  
**BANA → kill**

This is also the ancestor/cognate of English **bane**, as in:

> “the bane of someone”

meaning something that causes ruin or destruction. [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/bani?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

There is another Germanic family that looks almost identical but is historically treated separately:

**Old Norse banna** = forbid  
**Old High German bannan** = command / proclaim / forbid  
**German bannen** = banish, prohibit, drive away  
**English ban** = forbid

The older Germanic sense appears to have been **“speak publicly / proclaim / command,”** developing into “prohibit” or “put under a ban.” [Duden](https://www.duden.de/rechtschreibung/bannen?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Arabic بَنَى — **banā**

Arabic has a striking phonetic match:

**بَنَى — banā**

According to Ibn Fāris in *Maqāyīs al-Lugha*, the root is:

**ب ن ي — B-N-Y**

and its fundamental meaning is:

**“building something by joining one part to another.”**

He says:

> **الباء والنون والياء أصل واحد، وهو بناء الشيء بضم بعضه إلى بعض**

So:

**بَنَى banā** = he built  
**بناء bināʾ** = building/construction  
**بنيان bunyān** = structure/building [Islamweb](https://islamweb.net/ar/library/content/124/356/%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86-%D9%88%D9%85%D8%A7-%D9%8A%D8%AB%D9%84%D8%AB%D9%87%D9%85%D8%A7-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AB%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%AB%D9%8A?utm_source=chatgpt.com)


Therefore there is a fascinating sound comparison:

**Old Norse:**  
**bana** → kill  
**bani** → death / killer

**Germanic:**  
**banna / bannan** → proclaim → forbid / ban

**Arabic:**  
**banā بَنَى** → build, put parts together

But based on established historical linguistics, I would **not claim that Arabic بَنَى and Old Norse bani/bana are cognates** merely from the similarity. Their reconstructed histories and meanings are different.

One particularly interesting direction for your comparisons, though, is **B-N / B-N-Y versus Germanic B-N forms connected with “building, dwelling, binding, or joining.”** That may produce a much closer semantic match to Arabic **بنى** than Old Norse *bana* “kill.”
