If by **TA-BA-AA** you mean the consonantal pattern **T-B-ʿ / ت ب ع**, there is a very interesting semantic comparison, although I would **not call the Germanic words cognates** without historical sound evidence.

In **Arabic ت ب ع (T-B-ʿ)**, Ibn Fāris gives one basic meaning: **التَّلْو والقَفْو** — “to come after, follow behind, pursue someone’s track.” So **تَبِعَ** = “he followed,” **تابع** = follower, and **اتّبع** = follow/pursue. [Islamweb](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/124/514/%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D9%88%D9%85%D8%A7-%D9%8A%D8%AB%D9%84%D8%AB%D9%87%D9%85%D8%A7?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

| Language | Form | Meaning | Comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Arabic** | **تَبِعَ – tabiʿa** | follow, go after, trace someone | **Very close to movement behind another** |
| **German** | **tappen** | walk/pad cautiously, grope one's way | movement step-by-step |
| **Middle High German** | **tāpen** | source of German *tappen* | walking/groping |
| **English** | **tap** | strike/touch lightly; repeated small contact | weaker semantic resemblance |
| **Old Norse / Scandinavian** | related forms such as Swedish **tappa/tippa** are compared with German *tappen* | small/soft stepping motions | movement by repeated steps |

German **tappen** specifically means moving with soft or uncertain steps, or feeling one's way in the dark. Duden traces it through Middle High German **tāpe/tappen**, though its deeper origin is uncertain. [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/tappen?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

So there is an interesting conceptual overlap:

**Arabic T-B-ʿ**
→ follow  
→ go behind  
→ pursue a track  
→ one movement after another

versus Germanic **tap-/tapp-**
→ step/pad  
→ move cautiously  
→ repeated footsteps/contact.

The particularly interesting thing is that Arabic **تَبِعَ** is not simply “walking.” It specifically contains the idea of **one thing coming after another**. Ibn Fāris says the root is fundamentally about **following and tracking**. [Islamweb](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/124/514/%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D9%88%D9%85%D8%A7-%D9%8A%D8%AB%D9%84%D8%AB%D9%87%D9%85%D8%A7?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

So a conceptual reconstruction could look like:

**TA / TAB → step → successive steps → go after → FOLLOW**

But historically, I do **not** see evidence that German **tappen** or English **tap** comes from Arabic **تبع**. The resemblance is currently **phonetic + semantic**, not a demonstrated etymological relationship.

One more possibility is important: if by **TA-BA-AA** you intended Arabic **ط ب ع — Ṭ-B-ʿ (طَبَعَ)** rather than **ت ب ع**, that is a completely different root meaning **impress, stamp, imprint/nature**, and that gives us a different set of Germanic comparisons.
