Yes. There are several very close forms in Old Norse, Old High German, and Old English. The strongest matches are WALA / VALA, while VALI also exists in Old Norse.
Form	Language	Meaning
wala	Old High German	choice, selection → ancestor/relative of German Wahl “choice/election” Kiwix Server
wala	Old High German	also recorded among forms meaning well-being / prosperity, related to welo/wola Wiktionary
wala	Old English	probable/reconstructed root, base; heart/core of a matter; Bosworth-Toller records wala with uncertainty as “a root” Linguistics Research Center
wala	Old Saxon / Old Dutch	variant of wel/wela, “well, in a good manner” Wiktionary
vala	Old Norse	knuckle-bone, especially one that rolls; such bones were associated with divination/fortune-telling Old Norse Dictionary
Váli / Vali	Old Norse	personal/divine name; Váli, a son of Odin in Norse mythology Cleasby-Vigfusson Dictionary
val	Old Norse / Old Icelandic	choice, selection, related to velja “choose” Wikisource
wal / wala	Proto-West Germanic → OE/OHG	another separate family meaning slain, slaughter, battlefield; OE wæl, OHG wal Wiktionary


The most interesting group for your spelling is:
Old High German WALA = “choice”
→ Middle High German wal
→ modern German Wahl = “choice, election”
and compare Old Norse val = “choice” + velja = “to choose.” Wikisource
So there is a genuine old Germanic pattern:
WALA → WAL → WAHL
VAL → VELJA
with the central idea of choosing / selecting.
There is also a second Germanic family around WAL / WALA meaning power/control nearby: Old High German waltan means “to rule, wield, govern,” from which German words such as Gewalt developed. The historical dictionaries distinguish that root from the “choice” word, even though the forms are visually very close. Linguistics Research Center
So if you're investigating a form like WALI / VALI / WALA, the Germanic material is actually quite rich: choice/select, rule/wield, well-being, root/core, and Old Norse Váli all occur in closely similar forms.
