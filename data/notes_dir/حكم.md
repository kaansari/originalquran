The **most interesting comparison is Arabic حَكَمَ versus Germanic hemmen / Old Norse hemja**.

German **hemmen** means **“to check, hinder, restrain.”** Its older Germanic relative Old Icelandic **hemja** means **“to check, curb, restrain.”** One etymological source even gives the expression *hafa hemil á* in the sense of **“to restrain someone.”** [Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/An_Etymological_Dictionary_of_the_German_Language/Annotated/hemmen?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

That becomes interesting beside Arabic **حكم**, because the Arabic root isn't limited to abstract “wisdom”; its semantic field involves **judgment/control/restraint**. So conceptually you get:

**Arabic Ḥ-K-M → judgment → governing/control/restraint**

**Old Norse hemja → check → curb/restrain**

**German hemmen → check → hinder/restrain**

There is another Germanic family worth noticing: German **hegen**, from Middle High German *hegen* and Old High German *heggan*, originally **“to surround with a fence/enclose,”** later “keep, cherish, protect.” [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/hegen?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

So if you're looking for your recurring pattern of **similar consonants + related underlying concept**, **ḥakama / hemja / hemmen** is worth investigating. But I would **not claim an etymological relationship** from this evidence—the accepted histories currently treat the Germanic words independently.

That comparison may be much stronger than simply comparing modern “wisdom.”


Yes — **AKM** is much more interesting if you are thinking of **acumen**.

English **acumen** comes from Latin **acūmen**, pronounced roughly **ah-KOO-men**, meaning originally **“a sharp point”**, then figuratively **“sharpness of mind, insight, intelligence.”** Latin *acūmen* comes from *acuō* “to sharpen,” ultimately connected with the Indo-European root **\*ak-**, “sharp, pointed, piercing.” [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/acumen?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

There is an even closer **AKM** form in Greek:

| Form | Language | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **akmē (ἀκμή)** | Ancient Greek | point, edge, peak, highest point |
| **acūmen** | Latin | sharp point → mental sharpness |
| **acumen** | English | keen judgment, insight |
| **\*ak-** | Proto-Indo-European | sharp, pointed, pierce |

Greek **akmē** is especially striking because its consonantal frame is literally **A-K-M**. It meant **“point, edge, peak, prime.”** [Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/acme?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Now compare that to Arabic **ح-ك-م (Ḥ-K-M)**:

**حُكْم — ḥukm** = judgment/ruling  
**حَكِيم — ḥakīm** = wise / possessing sound judgment  
**حِكْمَة — ḥikmah** = wisdom  
**حَكَم — ḥakam** = judge/arbitrator

So semantically there is a very interesting overlap:

**AKM / acumen**
→ sharp point  
→ sharp perception  
→ mental sharpness  
→ good judgment

**ḤKM / حكم**
→ judgment  
→ deciding correctly  
→ wisdom  
→ governing/restraining

So **acumen ≈ mental sharpness/judgment**, while **ḥikmah / ḥukm ≈ wisdom/judgment**.

There is also an important sound observation. Greek **akmē** is pronounced approximately:

**ak-MAY / ak-MEH**

and Latin **acūmen**:

**a-KOO-men**

so the central consonantal sequence is indeed:

**K-M**

while Arabic gives:

**Ḥ-K-M**

That means **HKM could theoretically be compared with an older KM/AKM pattern if an initial ḥ/h were added, lost, or represented differently**—but there is currently **no accepted historical etymology connecting Arabic Ḥ-K-M with Indo-European \*ak- / Greek akmē / Latin acūmen**.

What makes your observation worth pursuing is **not just the sound**. It's the semantic path:

**“sharp point” → “sharp mind” → “discernment/judgment”**

which lands remarkably close to **حكمة / حكم**.

One more Germanic connection is useful: the same Indo-European **\*ak- “sharp”** family is associated with English **edge**, Old English **ecg**, Old Norse **egg** “edge/blade,” and German **Ecke** “corner.” [Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/acne?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

So a possible comparison map is:

**AK → sharp / edge / point**  
↓  
**AKMĒ → peak / sharp point**  
↓  
**ACUMEN → intellectual sharpness / discernment**  
↓  
**compare conceptually with ḤKM → judgment / wisdom**

That is a substantially stronger comparison than simply saying **HKM sounds like another Germanic word**. The **AKM → acumen → judgment/wisdom** semantic chain is genuinely interesting, even though a historical cognate relationship has not been demonstrated.
