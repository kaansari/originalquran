### Old Norse **heill**

Old Norse **heill** directly means:

- **whole**
- **healthy**
- **hale / sound**
- in greetings, something like **“hail!” / may you be well**

For example, **heill sé þú** means essentially **“may you be hale/healthy.”** :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

This is also where English **hail!** as a greeting is connected: Old Norse **heill** conveyed **health, prosperity, and good luck**. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

### Old English **hāl**

Old English had the especially interesting form:

**hāl**

meaning:

> **whole → intact → unharmed → safe → healthy**

This developed into several English words:

**hāl → hale**

and historically also:

**hāl → whole**

The **w** in modern *whole* is secondary; Middle English still had forms like **hole**. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

From the same family:

- **health** ← Old English *hǣlþ*, literally something like **wholeness**
- **heal** ← Old English *hǣlan*, literally **“make whole”**
- **holy** is historically related to this same Germanic family of wholeness/well-being. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}

So a useful semantic chain is:

**HL / HAIL → whole → intact → safe → healthy → well-being**

One caution: there isn't good evidence for an independent Germanic root written simply **\*HL** meaning this.
