Yield, Jiel

 There is a **very interesting sound + meaning parallel** around Arabic **جَعَلَ (jaʿala)** and Germanic **gjalda / geld / yield**, but they are **not considered proven cognates** by standard historical linguistics.

| Language | Word | Pronunciation | Core meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Arabic** | **جَعَلَ** | **ja-ʿa-la** | to make, put, appoint, cause something to become |
| **Old Norse** | **gjalda** | roughly **GYAL-da** | to pay, repay, return, render, give back |
| **Old Norse noun** | **gjald** | **GYALD** | payment, due, recompense |
| **German** | **gelten** | **GEL-ten** | to count as, be valid, be worth |
| **German** | **Geld** | **gelt** | money |
| **Old English** | **ġieldan / ġyldan** | roughly **YIL-dan** | to pay, render, give |
| **English** | **yield** | **yeeld** | to give, produce, render, surrender |

The Germanic family is quite secure:

**Proto-Germanic \*geldaną → Old Norse gjalda → German gelten / Geld → Old English ġieldan → English yield.** Its oldest reconstructed sense is connected with **paying, rendering, repaying, giving what is due**. [Old Norse Dictionary](https://oldnorsedictionary.com/home/word/gjalda?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

A good Old Norse example is:

**gjalda gjǫf við gjǫf**  
= “repay a gift with a gift” / “return gift for gift.” [Old Norse Dictionary](https://oldnorsedictionary.com/home/word/gjalda?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For Arabic **ج ع ل**, Ibn Fāris's *Maqāyīs al-Lugha* treats Arabic roots according to underlying semantic principles; **جعل** develops around the idea of **making/placing something into a state or position**. So Qurʾānic **جعل** can mean things such as “made,” “placed,” “appointed,” or “caused to become.” [Arabic Keyboard](https://arabic-keyboard.info/books/ar/read-book/%D9%85%D9%82%D8%A7%D9%8A%D9%8A%D8%B3-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%84%D8%BA%D8%A9?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The interesting overlap is therefore:

**جَعَلَ**
→ make / place / cause / assign

**gjalda / yield**
→ render / give / return / produce

So both can converge conceptually on:

> **causing something to be given, produced, assigned, or brought into a resulting state.**

There is also a striking sound comparison:

**JAʿALA**  
**GJALDA**

Ignoring the Germanic **-d-**, you get something visually close to:

**J-ʿ-L** ↔ **G-J-L-D**

And Old Norse initial **gj-** is pronounced roughly like a **y / gy** sound, so **gjalda** is approximately **“GYAL-da”**, not hard English “g-jalda.”

But the important distinction is: **Germanic gjalda is historically traced to Proto-Germanic \*geldaną and PIE \*gʰeldʰ- “pay.”** There is currently no accepted linguistic derivation connecting it directly to Arabic **ج ع ل**.
