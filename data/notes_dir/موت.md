Old Norse **mót** is particularly close phonetically to **M-W-T / MŌT**. It meant **“meeting, encounter, coming together, junction.”** [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/m%C3%B3t?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Old English also had **mōt**, “meeting/assembly,” from Proto-Germanic **\*mōtą**. This survives in English **moot**, originally a meeting or assembly. [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Reconstruction%3AOld_English/mot?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

There is a **different Germanic root**:

**Old High German muot → German Mut → English mood**

This root concerned **mind, spirit, disposition**, and German **Mut** developed the meaning **“courage.”** It comes from Proto-Germanic **\*mōdaz**, so it should not be confused historically with **\*mōtą “meeting.”** [Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Mut?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

So the strongest Germanic match to **MWT / م-و-ت** in **sound**, rather than meaning, is:

> **mōt / mót → encounter, meeting, coming together**
